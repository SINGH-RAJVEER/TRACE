# Documented industry deployment of distillation detection and prevention

## What have frontier providers actually deployed?

### Takeaway

The strongest public evidence is first-party incident reporting and deployment descriptions from Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI. Their public numerical results concern particular reasoning-extraction campaigns or protected-reasoning exposure; they do not establish general detection accuracy for benign-looking output harvesting. All dated incident articles below precede the research cutoff of 2026-11-05. These are provider-reported observations, not independently audited effectiveness studies. — [Anthropic February](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks), [Anthropic October](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [Google](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/distillation-experimentation-integration-ai-adversarial-use), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)

### Cited Findings

- **Anthropic, 2026-02-23; deployed campaign detection.** Reports >16 million exchanges across approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts. Uses traffic classifiers, behavioral fingerprints, chain-of-thought elicitation detection, and coordination detection; account verification and indicator sharing supplement these. Observed signals include repetitive prompt structure, capability concentration, volume, synchronized timing, shared payment methods, request metadata, IP/infrastructure correlation, and partner corroboration. One proxy network mixed unrelated traffic with harvesting across >20,000 accounts. Telemetry therefore spans content, account history, timing, payments, origin, and infrastructure. No detector precision/recall, decision thresholds, benign comparison cohort, or latency distribution is disclosed. Output-devaluation safeguards were described as development work at this date. — [Detecting and preventing distillation attacks](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- **Anthropic, 2026-10-12; deployed reasoning protection.** Mechanisms: summarized reasoning; context/model/account binding of reasoning; red-teamed two-stage extraction classifiers; rapid patches; stricter limits and bans for persistent attempts; charging blocked requests. Reports <0.1% classifier false positives, reasoning tokens harvested on its own platform reduced 10–100-fold, and tens of thousands of bans, often within two hours of campaign start. An internal SWE-bench Verified distillation experiment scored 80.4% using full versus 71.8% using summarized Opus 4.8 transcripts. These are different measurements: exposure reduction, classifier false positives, and student benchmark performance. The provider says sophisticated persistent bypass attempts have not yet tested these defenses fully. Public benchmark protocol, classifier evaluation population, confidence intervals, and campaign-ground-truth construction remain undisclosed. — [Strengthening our defenses against distillation attacks](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses)
- **Google DeepMind/GTIG, 2026-02-12; deployed but underspecified.** Reports frequent extraction attempts by private-sector entities and researchers, and real-time recognition of one campaign with >100,000 prompts targeting reasoning in non-English languages. Google says its systems reduced this particular attack's risk and protected reasoning traces. Reported response includes disabling accounts/projects and feeding intelligence back into classifiers/models. Requires prompt/campaign visibility and account/project identity; exact features, architecture, numerical detection quality, successful-extraction fraction, student fidelity, and benign impact are not published. The 100,000 figure is observed campaign scale, not a detector benchmark or evidence of 100,000 successful extractions. — [GTIG AI Threat Tracker](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/distillation-experimentation-integration-ai-adversarial-use)
- **OpenAI, 2026-09-30; deployed campaign disruption and reasoning-artifact isolation.** A July campaign replayed encrypted reasoning across conversations. July 24–25 spikes included 16,000 relevant-pattern requests from >4,000 users; investigation found a >15,000-user cluster disrupted by July 28. A footnote explicitly identifies these as attempted, not necessarily successful extractions. Controls include account restrictions/bans, signup/infrastructure controls, related-network monitoring, boundaries across users/workspaces/organizations/model families, replay-path closure, and detection/holding of streamed output exposing reasoning. Third-party-provider cooperation and Frontier Model Forum sharing supported response. Data needed: request patterns, user/network relationships, protected-artifact provenance, model identities, and streamed content. No general harvesting detector metrics or quantified residual leakage reduction are supplied. OpenAI states cloud-partner parity, tool-output protection, and classifier coverage remain ongoing work. Attribution of a core cluster to Moonshot-associated individuals does not establish that every operator shared one actor. — [Disrupting a coordinated model-distillation campaign](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)

### Inferences

- **Campaign attribution requires combined evidence.** Shared IP alone cannot establish malicious coordination: NATs, institutional egress, resellers, and legitimate shared infrastructure are alternative explanations. Use independently corroborating behavioral and identity evidence, retain uncertainty, and distinguish suspected links from confirmed campaign membership. This is an engineering inference from the multi-signal attribution methodology, not a published provider rule or validated threshold. — [Anthropic February](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks)
- **Ordinary output harvesting remains a separate evaluation target.** A hidden-reasoning-elicitation classifier can recognize conspicuous requests yet leave an attacker who only collects final answers untouched. TRACE should evaluate that threat directly instead of transferring the above reasoning-specific performance claims. — [Anthropic October](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)
- **Binding is prevention, not intent inference.** Enforcing artifact provenance can close an unauthorized replay path without deciding whether a normal-looking request is training-data acquisition. It applies when the product exposes reusable protected reasoning artifacts; it is not automatically relevant to a final-answer-only teacher. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)

### Gaps

- No evaluated public deployment here establishes a universal detector for authorized, low-rate, semantically diverse final-answer harvesting.
- Provider articles do not publish sufficient labeled data, cohort definitions, thresholds, calibration curves, or full detection-delay distributions to reproduce their operational detector performance.
- Attribution and student-training intent are generally provider assessments. Public reports do not expose complete evidence or a court finding proving each attributed campaign.

## Which infrastructure controls exist, and what do they prove?

### Takeaway

AWS, Microsoft, and Cloudflare document usable abuse-monitoring or traffic-control building blocks. The reviewed documentation does not validate those features as general LLM-distillation detectors. — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring), [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/rate-limiting/)

### Cited Findings

- **AWS Bedrock; documented service abuse process.** AWS may use automated detection against its or model-provider terms, request explanations, and suspend model/service access. Some model traffic is retained for automated offline review; specified classifier-flagged Claude traffic can receive human review. Retained content is processed by AWS rather than shared with third-party model providers. This is evidence of an operational abuse process, with account identity, classifier flags, and model input/output visibility; it discloses no extraction-specific features, accuracy, or distillation disruption results. The page is live and has no visible publication date in the retrieved text, so historical availability of every current retention detail by the cutoff is unverified. — [Amazon Bedrock abuse detection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html)
- **Microsoft Foundry/Azure OpenAI; updated 2026-05-19.** Documented pipeline combines harmful-content classification, customer usage-pattern algorithms/heuristics, frequency/severity/intentionality scoring, automated or authorized-human review, then notification/remediation or suspension. Examples center on harmful content and repeated jailbreaks. Telemetry includes prompts/completions, classification signals, and usage history. This demonstrates a layered abuse pipeline with review, not measured detection of benign-looking model extraction; no extraction-specific benchmark is given. Modified abuse monitoring changes review behavior. — [Abuse monitoring](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring)
- **Cloudflare AI Gateway; updated 2026-09-30.** Supports fixed/sliding request-count windows and rejects excess traffic with HTTP 429; default settings apply uniformly across the gateway. Needs request counts and timing, not semantic or training-intent inference. This can bound throughput, while low-rate or distributed harvesting remains a separate detection problem. The page provides no measured extraction-detector result. — [Rate limiting](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/rate-limiting/)

### Inferences

- TRACE can adopt count/time controls and review workflows as operational primitives while separately validating its actual extraction classifier. A rate limiter's rejection count is not a true-positive count; a harmful-content classifier's score is not calibrated distillation risk. — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/rate-limiting/), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring)
- Partner-hosted deployment has its own observability and enforcement boundary. A teacher vendor's first-party detector does not automatically imply equivalent visibility or coverage through cloud providers and aggregators. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/), [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html)

### Gaps

- No reviewed AWS, Azure, or Cloudflare source provides an extraction-specific confusion matrix, calibrated false-positive threshold, or student-fidelity outcome. This bounded search does not prove that these companies have no private detectors.
- Live documentation is mutable. Where a visible update date is absent, it should be cited as currently retrieved documentation rather than a frozen historical snapshot.

## What is guidance rather than demonstrated deployment?

### Takeaway

A government advisory is useful operational guidance, but its recommendations are neither a certified industry standard nor evidence that a particular detector has been deployed successfully. — [NSA announcement, 2026-09-08](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4592113/nsa-and-others-warn-china-based-ai-companies-are-distilling-us-frontier-ai-mode/)

### Cited Findings

- NSA's 2026-09-08 announcement confirms the joint NSA/CISA/FBI advisory on industrial-scale distillation and describes cross-model-provider, cloud-platform, and infrastructure distribution as a challenge. It recommends ecosystem collaboration and says the advisory supplies detection guidance, TTPs, and mitigation practices. This is a primary-source confirmation of guidance, not a deployment evaluation. — [NSA press release](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4592113/nsa-and-others-warn-china-based-ai-companies-are-distilling-us-frontier-ai-mode/)

### Inferences

- A defensible TRACE evaluation should separately report (1) prompt-level hidden-reasoning extraction, (2) final-answer collection, (3) cross-account campaign linkage, and (4) intervention outcomes. Suggested measurements are precision/recall at realistic benign prevalence, per-cohort false positives, requests/tokens leaked before action, latency to action, and student quality after equal-cost training. These are research-design recommendations, not industry-certified thresholds; the provider reports motivate the separation but do not supply transferable calibration. — [Anthropic October](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/)

### Gaps

- Direct retrieval of the original [CISA AA26-251A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-251a) and its [government-hosted PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Sep/08/2003992823/-1/-1/1/CSA_CHINA_BASED_AI_COMPANIES_MALICIOUS_DISTILLATION_AGAINST_US.PDF) returned HTTP 403. Search surfaced mirrored text containing more specific recommendations, but those details are deliberately excluded from primary-verified findings here. The NSA press release was fetched successfully.
- This task did not audit standards publications or validate any specific mitigation as a mandatory industry standard; the coordinator's standards research should establish that separately.
