# Detect extraction campaigns without inventing certainty

As of **2026-11-05**, public evidence supports layered campaign detection, account investigation, output-access controls and resource limits for protecting teacher APIs. It does **not** establish a universal detector of whether an API caller trains a student, or an industry certification for distillation-detection accuracy. Anthropic, Google and OpenAI describe operational defenses, but their public reports omit the data and calibration needed to reproduce general detection performance. The strongest quantified deployment claims concern attempts to obtain protected reasoning, which differs from collecting ordinary final answers. Research offers useful distribution, similarity and coordination detectors, usually under narrower experimental assumptions. TRACE should build on its existing signals with a **persistent, bounded, multiscale campaign detector**, calibrate the complete monitoring procedure against legitimate workflows, and require corroboration before punitive action. These are engineering recommendations derived from the evidence below, not a claim of already demonstrated TRACE effectiveness. ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [Google](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/distillation-experimentation-integration-ai-adversarial-use), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/), [NIST taxonomy](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ai/100/2/e2025/final))

## Providers detect campaigns and protect specific channels

Anthropic's February 2026 account describes more than 16 million exchanges involving approximately 24,000 fraudulent accounts. Its reported defenses combine traffic classifiers, behavioral fingerprints, reasoning-elicitation detection and coordination analysis with account verification and indicator sharing. Evidence includes repetitive request structures, capability concentration, timing, payment connections and infrastructure. A proxy network mixed harvesting with unrelated traffic across more than 20,000 accounts. This documents deployed investigation and enforcement, but supplies no reproducible precision, recall, legitimate-user cohort or decision threshold. ([Anthropic, February 23](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks))

Anthropic's October report describes deployed summarized reasoning, binding of reasoning to context/model/account, two-stage extraction classifiers, patches, limits, bans and charging blocked requests. Its **90–99% reduction, equivalently 10–100-fold, concerns harvested reasoning tokens on its own platform**; the reported classifier false-positive rate below 0.1% also belongs to this protected-reasoning defense. Neither number establishes accuracy against ordinary final-answer collection. Its internal student experiment reports 80.4% SWE-bench Verified with full versus 71.8% with summarized transcripts, supporting a channel-specific output-value intervention. Evaluation-population details and confidence intervals remain undisclosed; the provider says sophisticated persistent bypass efforts have not yet fully tested these defenses. ([Anthropic, October 12](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses))

Google reports real-time recognition of a campaign exceeding 100,000 prompts targeting reasoning in non-English languages. It describes account/project disruption and incorporating findings into defenses, without publishing detector architecture, calibrated error rates or student-fidelity outcomes. The request count describes campaign scale, not successful extraction. OpenAI describes replay of encrypted reasoning across conversations, investigation of a cluster exceeding 15,000 users, account disruption, cross-user/workspace/organization/model boundaries and detection or holding of reasoning-bearing streamed output. Its July 24–25 pattern counts concern **attempted, not necessarily successful, extraction**. Both reports support deployment claims for particular campaigns, not general-purpose detection benchmarks. ([Google, February 12](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/distillation-experimentation-integration-ai-adversarial-use), [OpenAI, September 30](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/))

Cloud services document useful supporting controls. Bedrock describes automated abuse review and possible service suspension; Azure describes classification, usage-pattern analysis, review and remediation; Cloudflare documents fixed/sliding request limits and HTTP 429 responses. None of these reviewed pages publishes an extraction-specific confusion matrix or student outcome. Bedrock's live page lacks a visible publication date, so its current retention details are not established as a historical snapshot at the cutoff. The NSA's September 2026 announcement confirms joint governmental distillation guidance and ecosystem collaboration, but the underlying advisory could not be retrieved in this research. Its detailed recommendations are therefore excluded. ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html), [Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring), [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai-gateway/features/rate-limiting/), [NSA](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Press-Releases-Statements/Press-Release-View/Article/4592113/nsa-and-others-warn-china-based-ai-companies-are-distilling-us-frontier-ai-mode/))

## Research supplies detectors with distinct assumptions

The following table separates observable mechanisms from claims about intent. "Research" means published experimental evidence, not production validation. Required telemetry is part of the deployment cost, especially when teacher internals or reliable account identities are necessary.

| Method | Mechanism and required telemetry | Evidence and status | Failure modes and TRACE relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Provider campaign classifiers | Combine request patterns, account history, timing, identity/payment and infrastructure relationships. | Vendor-reported deployment; general accuracy and thresholds undisclosed. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks) | Shared infrastructure, ordinary automation and mixed proxy traffic confuse attribution. Build corroborated campaign evidence. |
| Protected-reasoning controls | Detect elicitation or leaked reasoning; bind artifacts to authorized contexts; limit or summarize releases. Needs channel/provenance and sometimes streamed-output visibility. | Deployed, channel-specific defenses. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/) | Does not classify benign-looking final-answer harvesting. Relevant only if TRACE exposes a protected channel. |
| PRADA | Test nearest historical query-distance distributions for non-normality, conditioned on predicted classes; retains client history. | EuroS&P 2019 image-classifier experiments; starts after more than 100 retained distances. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.02628v3) | Normality and distance assumptions are task-specific; blinding evades related monitoring. Text adaptation needs fresh validation. |
| VarDetect | Encode training inputs and noisy outliers with a modified VAE; compare rolling user distributions using multi-kernel MMD. | Vision experiments with 100-query buffers and tested adaptive attacks. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05166) | Requires training inputs, encoder and stable identity; distribution shift need not mean extraction. Reuse the comparison idea, not vision thresholds. |
| SEAT | Count similar query pairs in an adversarially robust learned representation. Requires encoder and account query history. | AISec 2021 image extraction; tests augmentation, blinding and filtering. [Paper](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/seat-aisec21.pdf) | Diverse natural inputs avoid repeated-pair assumptions; legitimate editing creates similarity. Account-cost benefits assume costly replacement accounts. |
| HODA / FDINet | HODA compares checkpoint-derived hardness histograms; FDINet detects feature distortion against training anchors and links similar accounts. | Vision research; FDINet explicitly leaves language-model extension open. [HODA](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=eDjxhFbaWX), [FDINet](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11338v1) | Needs teacher checkpoints or internal features/training anchors. Poor first choice for a third-party teacher gateway. |
| Extraction-progress coverage | Estimate information gain with a local substitute or summarize decision-tree feature coverage across users/groups. | BigML decision-tree API experiments. [Kesarwani et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.07221) | Semantic breadth is also legitimate research. Decision-tree coverage does not transfer directly to language capability. |
| Semantic traffic MMD | Compare normalized text embeddings against benign references using multi-bandwidth RBF MMD; retain window and reference batches. | June 2026 preprint; default 1,500-query windows, dataset-based experiments. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05725v1) | Topic/source mismatch, dilution, drift and large-window delay; window labels cannot identify individual malicious requests. |
| Distributed coordination | Summarize accounts with random-Fourier features, whiten against history, select shared deviations spectrally and remove diagonal self-concentration. | October 2026 preprint with later-week WildChat tests and small-student experiments. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13083v1) | Benign evaluation sweeps also trigger; weak small-colluder coverage, poisoning and relay failures. Use as corroboration. |
| Session classifier | Learn from trailing account/session aggregates, request structure, perplexity, coverage changes and infrastructure. | September 2026 technical report evaluated on generated sessions. [Report](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38239v1) | Generator labels can encode detector heuristics. Synthetic success does not qualify real campaign recognition. |
| Sequential statistical tests | Accumulate betting evidence or use time-uniform kernel tests; requires a defined null and predictable sampling/update rules. | Statistical theory, not LLM extraction deployment. [Betting](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.09162v6), [kernel testing](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/podkopaev23a/podkopaev23a.pdf) | Optional-stopping guarantees depend on assumptions; arbitrary drift, overlapping windows and model selection cannot be ignored. |
| Post-hoc distillation attribution | Compare suspect-model likelihoods on teacher reasoning against distilled/independent shadows. Requires suspect access and identifiable training queries. | October 2026 preliminary poster preprint; conference presentation after cutoff. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12137v1) | Answers model provenance after release, rather than identifying active clients. Watermarks/fingerprints likewise need separate attribution evidence. |

The semantic-MMD preprint reports 0.3% benign-window false positives, 100% pure-attacker detection and 59% detection at 5% contamination across its tested pairs. These are **large-window dataset results**, not production account-level guarantees. Its incoming score averages reference comparisons while threshold construction uses unaveraged comparisons, which further prevents treating a nominal percentile as a formal deployment guarantee. ([Semantic MMD](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05725v1))

The coordination preprint reports strong detection of some naive and padded campaigns, but real false alarms sometimes exceed its nominal 1%. It finds no deployable detector at colluder shares of 2.5% or below, and leaves alarm delay for its hollowed selection unmeasured. The session-classifier report's AUPRC falls from 0.993 against oracle labels to 0.313 against operational labels, both on synthetic sessions. These limitations make independent legitimate-user calibration a design requirement rather than a final reporting exercise. ([Coordination](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13083v1), [session classifier](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38239v1))

Natural-data extraction also matters. ActiveThief uses active learning over public unlabeled inputs, so near-duplicate and out-of-distribution detectors cover only some attacks. Query blinding has empirically defeated PRADA in an image adversarial-example setting; that result illustrates fragile distance assumptions without proving LLM extraction failure. Karchmer's conditional impossibility result concerns specified decision-tree concept classes, not all practical LLM defenses. The narrower operational limit is straightforward: **identical observable transcripts provide no passive evidence that separates two different downstream uses**. That last statement is an inference about observability, not an extrapolated theorem. ([ActiveThief](https://www.csa.iisc.ac.in/~vg/papers/aaai2020/aaai2020.pdf), [query blinding](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/stateful-spai20.pdf), [formal limit](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1039.pdf))

The reviewed authorities do not prescribe an extraction classifier, universal threshold or certified prevention rate. Their authority differs: an ISO management-system requirements standard, a voluntary NIST framework and an OWASP risk list support different claims. ## TRACE needs a persistent detector that acts on campaigns

Repository findings in the research notes show that TRACE already has domain-conditional MMD, rarefied coverage and behavioral signals, plus offline linkage/fusion tooling. The shadow monitor is process-local; restart resets its history. The experimental path calibration binds an exact look schedule and finite horizon, marks decisions `qualified: false`, and lacks independent benign and teacher/student effectiveness evidence. Admission already supports identity mappings, SQLite reservations, aggregate budgets and human review, although state defaults to memory and organization mappings are operator-supplied. These are implementation facts, not proof of live efficacy. ([Harness documentation](../harness.md), [shadow implementation](../../packages/harness/src/harness/shadow.py), [signals implementation](../../packages/harness/src/harness/signals.py), [evaluation protocol](../evaluation.md))

**Priority one is an actual online campaign detector with durable bounded state.** The following is original engineering synthesis. Add a versioned campaign-scoring module beside admission, backed by the persistent store. Track principals and verified organizations separately from tentative account clusters. Maintain bounded recent embedding buffers, longer-lived feature sketches and count/time summaries rather than retaining every prefix. Score several request-count and wall-clock scales so short bursts and slow accumulation both contribute. Persist update positions, references, score state, policy version, resets and alerts atomically; make retries idempotent. Record attempted requests separately from outputs actually released. Cap memory, storage and per-request compute, and define behavior when encoding fails or state is unavailable. Persistence must preserve accumulated evidence without allowing historical state to grow indefinitely.

The scorer should combine the existing conditional MMD/coverage evidence with two distinct sequence families: unusually repetitive semantic/paraphrase exploration and unusually systematic broad acquisition. Include timing, template changes, answer-only preference and released-token totals as contextual evidence. No one feature, including corpus matches or high diversity, should constitute a training-intent verdict. Store component contributions and missingness with each alert. Start with a transparent frozen score and fixed thresholds; adding a learned classifier before obtaining independently investigated labels risks teaching it the synthetic workload generator. This recommendation borrows stateful comparison and similarity mechanisms while explicitly requiring LLM-specific validation. ([VarDetect](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05166), [SEAT](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~daw/papers/seat-aisec21.pdf), [synthetic-label limitation](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38239v1))

**Priority two is calibration of that complete persistent procedure.** Construct legitimate references matched by task/domain, endpoint, language, workload size and workflow, with documented fallback or abstention when coverage is insufficient. Include researchers, benchmark sweeps, labeling jobs, tutoring, retries, batch automation and shared organizations. Separate fitting, score calibration, policy selection and final testing by source family and account/organization. Calibrate the maximum alert statistic over the actual multiscale look schedule, reset policy, account/group search and operating horizon. A fixed-window rank does not control months of repeated monitoring. A genuine sequential test is a later option only when its null and predictability assumptions fit operations; ordinary ranks must not be renamed an e-process. ([Time-uniform testing](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/podkopaev23a/podkopaev23a.pdf), [betting framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.09162v6), [conditional tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03898))

**Priority three is multiaccount corroboration and proportionate response.** Add centered, normalized account-direction summaries and bounded candidate-cluster searches. Compare synchronized semantic deviations with identity, timing and independently verified infrastructure evidence. Preserve confidence and provenance for every link; shared IP addresses alone cannot establish common malicious ownership. Evaluate false merges on benign organizations and coordinated legitimate evaluation campaigns. Initially send corroborated alerts to review, temporary limits and verification with appeal/restoration records. Keep confirmed organization membership distinct from statistical clusters. This is a proposed operational use of coordination research, not a reproduction of its published performance. ([Coordination study](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13083v1), [provider campaign evidence](https://www.anthropic.com/news/detecting-and-preventing-distillation-attacks), [AI RMF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf))

**Priority four is a narrow protected-channel defense, if that channel exists.** Train a request classifier for specific forbidden reasoning-elicitation/replay patterns, enforce artifact provenance and inspect applicable release paths. Calibrate separately against legitimate explanations and debugging requests. A final-answer-only gateway should prioritize campaign behavior instead; a generic prompt classifier cannot observe downstream training intent. Preserve durable quotas, rate/concurrency controls and exposure limits as independent acquisition controls, with separate decision reasons. Budget refusals are not detector true positives. ([Reasoning protection](https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses), [artifact boundaries](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign/), [resource guidance](https://api-security.owasp.org/editions/2023/en/0xa4-unrestricted-resource-consumption/))

## Conclusion

The decisive engineering question is whether the new scorer produces useful campaign evidence beyond inexpensive resource controls at the same legitimate-user burden. Test account rotation, low-and-slow collection, padding, task-matched prompts, reference poisoning, restarts and post-alert in-flight releases. Report precision at realistic prevalence, account/campaign false-alert probability over the declared horizon, abstention, detection delay, tokens released before action, review cost and restored-user utility. Then train students on actually released outputs with matched-volume and matched-cost controls. Until that last step succeeds, the supported claim is reduced acquisition or measured detection under tested alternatives, not prevented model replication. This proposed evidence boundary follows TRACE's existing evaluation protocol and the separation between monitoring and capability claims in the research. ([TRACE protocol](../evaluation.md), [coordination/student testbed](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13083v1))

TRACE's useful contribution would be a reproducible detector with explicit memory bounds, identity scope, legitimate comparison populations and intervention consequences. The absence of a universal intent oracle makes those boundaries more valuable: they let another operator determine exactly which campaigns the system detects, which controls merely slow acquisition, and where its evidence ends.
